Get It on Steam

Version 2.318.0

Esc leaves your Redo alone

Press Esc to cancel a floating piece, a Text or Shape you are placing, a pen path or a key you drag in the Graph panel, and Undo and Redo stay exactly as they were, so a step you undid is still there to bring back.

A locked layer stays locked

Grayscale, Posterize, Snap to Palette, Gradient Map, Add Outline, Replace Color and the other commands that change a whole layer now leave a locked layer alone and say why, the way the brushes always have. A macro's color steps skip a locked layer too.

More of your work counts as unsaved

Changing a guide, the color Budget, Batch Export's re-run on save, Palette Swap's export variants, the tileset or an effect layer's settings now marks the picture unsaved, so closing asks before they are lost.

Fixes for undo, selections and tiles

Undo or Redo in the middle of a drag now asks you to finish it first, a tool key in the middle of a drag lands what you drew, Delete, Cut, Paste and Fill say why when a layer cannot take them instead of adding an empty step, and capturing a tile is its own undo step.

Version 2.317.4

Bigger letters in the Font panel

Each letter button in the Font panel is now its full size, with the letter filling it in the accent green and a drawn letter showing your art in the corner. On the sheet, the empty cells' letters and the cell you are drawing stand out in the same green.

Labels and titles read the same way everywhere

Section titles, dialog titles, menu items, buttons and the labels beside controls now share one capitalization, so Pen and Dynamics reads like the menus around it.

Version 2.317.3

Fullscreen every time, and a close button

The app opens in fullscreen every time, even after an update, unless Preferences, Workspace, Open the app says Maximized. The ✕ at the top right closes it in one click, the bottom-right corner shows F11 for fullscreen and switches with a click, and Esc now only cancels what you are doing.

Version 2.317.0

New and Open ask to save your work first

File ▸ New, Open, Open Recent and the other doors that replace your picture now offer to save unsaved changes first, the way closing does. Settings ▸ Ask to Save Before Closing turns both off.

Lock the guides in place

View ▸ Guides ▸ Lock Guides holds every guide line where it is until you turn it off, and pressing on a locked guide pans the view instead of moving it.

A crosshair cursor

Preferences ▸ Editing ▸ Crosshair Cursor swaps the plain arrow for a cross while the pointer is over the canvas.

A grayscale picture offers its own grays

Open a picture that is all grays and it offers a palette of exactly the grays it uses, up to 32, unless your palette is already gray. A Don't ask again tick turns the offer off.

Fixes for saving, undo, gray pictures and zooming out

Saving while a lifted piece is still floating now stamps it down first, so the file has it. Dragging the Tile Stamp with no tile picked no longer deletes earlier undo steps, a gray picture saved at 16 bits opens in its own shades instead of almost white, and at zooms below 1:1, dragging beside the picture pans again.

Version 2.316.0

Imported photos travel with the project

A photo you import as a layer is now saved with the project, in full, and so are a painted layer's finest pixels, so the Pixel size stays crisp after you reopen it. The file grows by about the photo's own size.

Pixelate Mode can trace over a reference image

File ▸ Import Reference Image… now works in Pixelate Mode too: pin a picture to trace over, dimmed, locked and never exported.

Version 2.315.0

The canvas size, always in view

The picture's width × height now sits at the bottom right, beside the zoom, and follows every change. Click it to resize the canvas.

Drag the canvas edges to resize it

Eight small handles sit just outside the canvas, on its corners and sides. Drag one to add or remove room on that side: the art stays the same size and in place, and one undo puts it back.

Pixel size works on a picture you started blank

Pixel size now makes the pixels chunkier on a new picture the way it does on an opened photo: a photo you imported as a layer re-pixelates crisply from the photo, painted pixels coarsen and usually come back when you lower it again, and moved or animated layers stay in place.

A big picture leaves the canvas on screen

Live Preview and Context Preview draw a big picture smaller to fit their panel, instead of pushing the canvas off the bottom of the screen.

Version 2.314.0

Fast strokes land as unbroken lines

A quick flick now paints the same unbroken line a slow stroke does, instead of dots with gaps between them: with the brush, the eraser, every ink and a captured brush, whose spacing is now measured along the line. A pixel-perfect or tapered stroke that leaves the canvas and comes back no longer draws a straight line across your art.

Drop an animated GIF to add its frames

Drop a GIF, APNG or animated WebP onto an open picture and it offers to add its frames, each timed the way the file says, or to add it as one layer. One undo takes it all back.

Version 2.313.0

Pixel Doctor shows every change before it makes it

Pixel Doctor's fix for lone pixels now removes only a tiny stray floating on its own, up to three pixels touching nothing, and blends in a speck that is almost the same color as the art around it. Every other single pixel, an eye, a glint, a highlight, is left alone, and so are soft shadows and glows. Before you press Fix, the canvas shows every pixel it will change; with a selection it works only inside it, and Ctrl+Z undoes it from the Pixel Doctor window. Snapping to the palette is offered only when at least half the picture already uses it.

Version 2.312.0

Reset Tool Options really resets every knob

One click puts every knob on the tool card back to its default, the captured brush's included: its mode, scale, turn, flips, spacing, jitter, frame order, All Frames and Follows the Stroke; the dither brush's pattern, lays and fill; blur's Keep to Palette; pollen; push; the isometric face, shades, shade faces, blend and material; close gaps; pen width; the select shape; and the material with its Chameleon mode. Your colors stay, and so does the brush you captured.

System fonts by name now work on Linux

The Text tool lists the fonts installed on the computer, the way it always has on Windows.

A keyed layer's slider and spins set a key

On a layer with keys in the timeline, the Opacity slider now sets a Fade key where Fade has keys, and each Offset spin sets an Across or Down key where that row has keys, instead of being ignored. One drag is one undo step, and when you pick such a layer the bar says which control sets keys.

Version 2.311.0

The brush tapers, and a captured brush turns with the stroke

Taper, in Tool Options for Paint and Erase, thins both ends of a stroke over the cells you set, so a line starts thin, swells, and ends thin again; the preview shows it as you draw. Follows the stroke, a box under the captured brush rows in Tool Options, turns the captured art to face the way your hand moves, in quarter turns.

Version 2.310.0

The brush can change as you stroke

Preferences, Pen and Dynamics now sets what the brush's size, its fade and its new dither density follow: nothing, a pen's pressure, how fast your hand moves, or how far the stroke has travelled. Faster and further thin the brush; a pen presses harder for more. Everything starts at nothing, so the brush paints as it always has until you choose.

A picture that truly has one frame still says so.

Batch and sheet exports no longer follow a paused playhead

Batch Export's PNGs, the animation sheet's collision outlines and Record to Frames no longer change when playback is paused mid-frame, and an Aseprite file's tags cover every frame the file holds when a layer has its own time.

The normal-map sheet follows a layer's own time

The Packed Atlas's normal-map sheet now has one cell per exported frame, each at its own moment, so it lines up with the picture sheet on a document with a timed layer. The note that said otherwise is gone.

Aseprite export works with tags again

Exporting a document that has tags to an .ase file used to fail; it works now, and the tags come across with their colors.

Version 2.298.0

Play just one layer's own loop

Every dialog fits a short screen

Version 2.297.0

A captured brush scales, turns and spaces out

Pick a captured brush in the Paint tool's options and the × spin scales it up to eight times, whole pixels only. Turn and the two Flip buttons reshape it on the spot, and two- or four-way radial symmetry turns each repeat instead of stamping it flat. Spacing and Jitter turn a steady drag into dashes or a scattered spray, and an animated brush can cycle its frames or shuffle them. The bracket keys step the scale, or the plain brush's size with nothing armed.

Inks paint through your own brush

A captured brush painting In my color answers to the Ink dropdown the way the plain brush does: Replace recolors matching pixels under the shape and Shade steps them along the ramp, cell by cell. As drawn still sets the ink aside and says so, since a multicolor stamp has no single color for an ink to change.

Capture an animation as a brush

An All Frames checkbox beside Capture reads the selection off every frame of the active layer and saves two or more as one animated brush, each dab painting the next. Import brush… on the picker's menu brings in a PNG or GIF from anywhere, and every brush in the list shows a small thumbnail of its own art.

Selections shift in place and save by name

With a selection on the canvas, Ctrl+Arrow moves the pixels under it one cell (five with Shift) while the marquee stays put, so a placement can be tried and shifted back. Edit ▸ Save Selection… stores the selection under a name and Edit ▸ Load Selection… brings any saved one back, even after the project is closed and reopened. The Select tool's options now offer a shape marquee too: Circle, Star, Heart and more.

An animated GIF opens with every frame

A GIF, APNG or animated WebP with several frames opens as that many frames, each timed the way the file says. It used to take the first frame and drop the rest without a word. The pixel size and sampling you set apply to every frame.

Version 2.287.0

Skew - lean your art

It asks for pixels, not degrees - and that is on purpose

A slant of 7° does not exist in pixel art, so a degrees box would round it silently and two different numbers would give you the same picture. Skew asks for one pixel of lean every so many rows, shows the angle that works out to, and cannot be asked for a slant it cannot draw.
